Node-link visualization

Drag stations to rearrange the graph. Hover over stations or routes for details and highlighting. Labels show station IDs; tooltips give full names. Positions are not geographic.

Node-link design

District determines node color; symbol area is proportional to daily passengers, emphasizing busy stations. Circles, diamonds, and squares distinguish Local, Transfer, and Terminal stations. Thicker links mean longer travel times, not stronger connections; dash patterns distinguish services. Dragging separates overlaps, hover highlights neighbors, and tooltips identify stations and routes.

Adjacency matrix

Gray cells mean no direct connection. Blue = Metro, orange = Express, purple = Shuttle; darker cells mean longer travel times. Hover for exact details.

Matrix design

Rows and columns are ordered by district, then station number; colored labels identify districts. Symmetric filled cells show undirected connections. Cell color preserves service type and opacity encodes travel time. District boundaries expose within- and between-district patterns. Station tooltips retain passenger volume and function without crowding the cells.

Network questions and findings

  1. Central stations — Partially, node-link: neighbor highlighting identifies s4, s7, s10 and eleven others with maximum degree three. There is no dominant hub; degree does not measure shortest-path importance.
  2. District connections — Well, matrix: the East–South blocks contain eleven unique connections, the most between districts. Symmetric cells represent each connection twice.
  3. Station roles — Well, node-link: diamonds appear at branches and leaves (s47, s50). Terminal squares include branching stations (s4, s7) and isolated stations (s43, s46, s49); function does not determine degree.
  4. Passenger volume — Well, node-link: s50, s49 and s48 have the largest symbol areas. Tooltips confirm 9,850, 9,677 and 9,504 daily passengers; high volume does not imply many connections.
  5. Longest connections — Well, both: s7–s31 is the thickest link and darkest filled matrix pair. Its tooltip identifies an Express connection taking sixteen minutes.
  6. Service concentrations — Partially, matrix: colored cells show Metro and Shuttle most frequently between East–South (four each). Express ties Central–North and East–West (four each). Services remain spread across several district pairs.
